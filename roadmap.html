The Real Gold Rush: Keeping It Alive

Generating documents is not the hard part. Keeping them synchronized is.

Company changes AI provider ↓ Inventory updates ↓ Risk Register updates ↓ Contracts update ↓ Transparency Register updates ↓ Training obligations update ↓ Evidence updates ↓ Everything remains synchronized

Then Comes Verification

Instead of an auditor asking "show me your AI Policy," they ask: "Prove that employees received AI literacy training." The system answers with structured evidence — employee records, timestamps, signed acknowledgements, logs, policies, affected AI systems, responsible persons. No searching through PDFs.

Then RRVI™

This is where the architecture becomes different. Instead of exchanging documents back and forth, the regulator sends a secure verification request directly to the organization's system, which returns structured evidence and cryptographic proof — resulting in a verification report. The regulator verifies remotely.

A different model This is a completely different model from today's document-exchange compliance process — verification happens against a live, structured system of record, not a folder of PDFs.

The Long-Term Progression

70 documents ↓ Conversation ↓ Parameterization ↓ Knowledge graph ↓ Evidence graph ↓ Compliance graph ↓ Continuous monitoring ↓ Remote verification ↓ Digital Trust Infrastructure ↓ European compliance infrastructure

The Biggest Opportunity

The 70 documents are the entry ticket, not the destination. The defensible asset is the structured compliance knowledge model underneath them: every obligation modeled, every document generated from that model, every parameter reused consistently, every piece of evidence linked, every update propagated automatically, every regulatory requirement traceable.

That transforms the product from a document generator into a compliance operating system.

Frequently asked questions

Is generating the 70 documents the end goal of EU AI Act Ready™?
No — it's described as the entry ticket, not the destination. Generating a complete compliance package in minutes is the first milestone; keeping it synchronized over time is the harder, ongoing problem.
What happens after a firm generates its 70-document pack?
The roadmap focuses on keeping documentation alive — evidence that stays current as the organization's AI systems and practices change, rather than a one-time snapshot.
How is this different from a chatbot that answers compliance questions?
Producing a complete, structured documentation package on demand goes beyond a chatbot — but the roadmap treats that as a baseline, not the finish line.
What kind of evidence does the system aim to produce over time?
Structured evidence — for example, instead of an auditor asking to see an AI Policy document, the system can answer 'prove employees received AI literacy training' with actual structured records.
Why do most SMEs currently have zero AI Act documentation?
The roadmap frames this as the market gap the product addresses — most SMEs haven't started, and generating the core document set (Policy, Inventory, Risk Assessment, Human Oversight, Incident Procedure, AI Literacy Plan) is the first real step.
Is the roadmap only about the EU AI Act?
The near-term roadmap centers on the EU AI Act, framed as the first regulation this infrastructure is built for.
What is the competitive advantage being built here?
Not the document generation itself, but keeping documentation continuously synchronized and evidenced — described as the harder, more defensible part of the product.
Does the roadmap cover pricing changes?
The roadmap page focuses on product direction — for current pricing, see the Generator and Partnership & Distribution pages.
Is the roadmap publicly updated?
The roadmap page is the public-facing summary of product direction as it's being built.
What's the relationship between the roadmap and RRVI™?
RRVI™ is the underlying architecture; the roadmap describes how that architecture is being extended over time, starting with the EU AI Act.
